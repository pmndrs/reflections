Reflections that flicker look worse than reflections that are a little off. A still image with a small error is
easier to live with than one that boils. The library's defaults lean toward stable, and this page says where the
dials are.

## Why it shimmers

Rough glossy surfaces send a random ray each frame. Each frame's answer is slightly different, so the raw signal is
noise. Three things calm it:

1. **The denoiser** blends the signal over several frames.
2. **Anti-aliasing and the upscaler** accumulate jittered frames of the final image.
3. **Fewer random choices**, which means fewer rays that change from frame to frame.

If something flickers, first check that the denoiser is actually attached. Without it the reflections shimmer a lot
(see [Troubleshooting](/guides/troubleshooting#reflections-flicker-or-look-grainy)).

## Settings, from the safest

| Setting | What you get | What it costs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anti-aliasing at ratio 1 (the upscaler as TAA) | Settles edges, normal-mapped highlights and the last shimmer of the denoised reflection. | The upscaler's own pass time. |
| `maxAccumulation` (upscaler) | A longer history is calmer at rest. | Ghosting lasts longer in motion. |
| `sharpness` (upscaler RCAS) | Keep it at 0 for reflections. Sharpening each jitter phase differently made mirror-edge flicker worse in the measurements. | A softer image. |
| The denoiser's `temporalStabilityFactor` | A higher value keeps the history longer. | Slower to follow change. |
| `mirrorBias` (default 0.5, with compensation) | Narrows the sampled lobe toward the mirror direction: less noise. | A sharper reflection that is a little biased. Compensation reduces the bias. |
| `samplesPerQuad: 4` | More rays, less noise. | More rays to trace. |
| `quadCopy: 'sameSurface'` | A quad stops sharing one ray across a silhouette, which removes bright dashes at edges. | A bit more work at edges. |
| Reactive mask (upscaler) | Less reflection ghosting where reflections move with the pixel. | More of their noise shows. |

## Settings that trade colour for stability

These are the ones to know about, because they make the picture steadier by making it less exact:

- **`mirrorBias`** truncates the reflection lobe. Higher is quieter and less correct.
- **A longer denoiser history** blurs fast change in exchange for calm.
- **The denoiser's hit-source options** (`denoiserHitSource`, `separateHitSources`, `missesMatchAnySource`) keep screen
  hits, traced hits and misses from blending. They improved a car's sill band, but the band's orbit flicker rose by
  about 2.5 times in the measurements, so the `performance` preset leaves them off. Flicker ranks first.
- **`reflectionResolution: 'half'`** is steady but loses fine detail.
- **A higher `roughnessThreshold`** traces more pixels. They are noisier, so the denoiser has more to do.

## A working recipe

1. Run the denoiser. Match its roughness gate to the tracer's threshold.
2. Use the upscaler at ratio 1 for anti-aliasing, with `sharpness` at 0 and a long `maxAccumulation`.
3. Keep `mirrorBias` and its compensation at the defaults.
4. If a thin feature still twinkles, try `samplesPerQuad: 2` or `quadCopy: 'sameSurface'`.
5. Judge it on a still camera and on a slow orbit. The two behave differently.

The [tuning bench](/examples/showcase/bench.html) (source: `examples/showcase/bench.html` in the repository) puts every
setting on one panel with a flicker measurement. See the [settings reference](/reference/settings) for each knob, its default and its
trade-off.
