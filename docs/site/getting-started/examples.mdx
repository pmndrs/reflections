## Features

One thing each, over a baseline you can switch back to.

### Box-projected probe

One baked probe in a room, projected onto the walls: reflections stay where they belong as you move.

**What it shows.** Why a probe is box-projected: without it the windows and walls reflected in the glossy floor sit in the wrong place and slide as the camera moves.

**Compare.** Box projection off: the same probe looked up by direction only (reflections at infinity)

**Try this.**

- Untick Box projection and orbit: the window reflections slide across the floor
- Switch the view to Floor and toggle again
- Show the volumes: the projection box is the room's walls

[Open the live page](/examples/features/probes/box-projection/) · [Source](https://github.com/pmndrs/reflections/blob/main/examples/features/probes/box-projection/main.ts) · [Docs](/concepts/reflection-probes)

### Two rooms

A probe per room and one in the doorway, blended by their influence boxes: each room reflects itself.

**What it shows.** Why a level needs more than one probe: each room reflects its own walls and light, and the doorway probe blends one room into the other without a seam.

**Compare.** One probe for both rooms: captured in the warm room, projected onto the whole building

**Try this.**

- Untick One probe per room: the cool room's floor and the mirror ball now show the warm room
- Show blend weights, then pick Across the doorway: the colours mix where the boxes overlap
- Watch the mirror ball roll through the doorway: its reflection hands over from one room to the other

[Open the live page](/examples/features/probes/two-rooms/) · [Source](https://github.com/pmndrs/reflections/blob/main/examples/features/probes/two-rooms/main.ts) · [Docs](/concepts/reflection-probes)

### Live probes

Probes re-captured while the scene runs, one per frame: a turning knot and two colour-cycling lights in two rooms.

**What it shows.** Probes that follow the scene: captured again, one probe per frame, as the lights change colour and the knot turns, so the reflections stay current.

**Compare.** Live capture off: the same probes baked once at load

**Try this.**

- Untick Live capture: the floor and the mirror ball keep the light colours of the moment the page loaded
- Set Probes per frame to all 3: the same image at three times the capture cost

[Open the live page](/examples/features/probes/live/) · [Source](https://github.com/pmndrs/reflections/blob/main/examples/features/probes/live/main.ts) · [Docs](/concepts/reflection-probes)

### Keep a mirror out of its own probe

bake(&#123; exclude &#125;): a moving mirror kept out of the live probe it reflects, so it does not paint itself onto the room.

**What it shows.** Why moving mirrors are kept out of the probes: captured, the knot is painted onto the wall and the floor, and the mirror floor shows a second, smeared knot where none is.

**Compare.** Without exclude: the knot is captured into the probe and painted onto the room

**Try this.**

- Untick Exclude the mirror knot: a ghost knot slides across the floor's reflection of the wall
- Untick Move, tick Show the probe and orbit: the ghost is the knot as seen from the capture point (the mirror sphere), painted onto the wall

[Open the live page](/examples/features/probes/exclude/) · [Source](https://github.com/pmndrs/reflections/blob/main/examples/features/probes/exclude/main.ts) · [Docs](/concepts/reflection-probes)

### Screen-space reflections

Screen-space reflections on a polished floor, over a probe: the objects stand on their reflections.

**What it shows.** What screen-space reflections add over a probe: exact, contact-sharp reflections of everything on screen, including the objects a probe leaves out.

**Compare.** Screen-space reflections off: the probe alone (three's lit image of the same frame)

**Try this.**

- Untick Screen-space reflections: the sphere and the box lose their reflections, the wall keeps its own
- Raise Floor roughness: the reflection blurs with distance from the contact, and the denoiser keeps it steady
- Pick At the sphere: the floor reflects its underside, which the camera cannot see (the probe fills it)

[Open the live page](/examples/features/ssr/mirror-floor/) · [Source](https://github.com/pmndrs/reflections/blob/main/examples/features/ssr/mirror-floor/main.ts) · [Docs](/concepts/screen-space-reflections)

### What screen space misses

Off screen, behind the camera, hidden: what screen-space reflections cannot see, and the probes filling it in.

**What it shows.** Where screen-space reflections run out: things off screen, behind the camera or hidden behind other objects. The probes fill those pixels so the image stays whole.

**Compare.** Screen space alone, without the probe fill: black where a ray found nothing on screen

**Try this.**

- Untick Fill misses from the probes: the ring in the floor and the whole wall mirror go black
- Tick Tint the misses: magenta marks how much of each smooth pixel the probes filled
- Pick Behind the camera: the mirror shows the bars behind you only through the probe

### Ray-traced fallback

Rays fill what screen space misses: reflections of things above the frame and behind the camera.

**What it shows.** What the ray-traced fallback adds over screen space + probes: sharp reflections of things off the screen, in the right place, including objects the probe never captured.

**Compare.** Ray-traced fallback off (`reflections.ssr.rayTracing = false`): the misses take the probe

**Try this.**

- Untick Ray-traced fallback and watch the wall mirror: the cylinder and the box vanish from it (the probe never saw them) and the bars go soft
- Switch the view to Wall mirror and toggle again
- Show where each pixel's reflection comes from: blue is a traced ray, green screen space, red the probe

### Ray tracing moving meshes

reflections.trace.refit() each frame keeps the ray-traced reflections of moving meshes where the meshes are.

**What it shows.** Why moving meshes need `trace.refit()` each frame: without it the rays still hit them where they stood at load, so their reflections lag or ghost.

**Compare.** Refit off: the BVH keeps the pose it was built with (stale reflections)

**Try this.**

- Untick Refit: the traced part of the knot's and the ball's reflections stays where they started
- Show traced pixels: those are the ones a stale BVH gets wrong
- Untick Animate to hold a pose and compare

### Animation and camera cuts

A camera path, moving objects and a camera cut with resetHistory().

**What it shows.** A whole animated scene, camera path and moving objects, steady through the moves; `resetHistory()` at the camera cuts and only there.

**Compare.** The history reset on every camera move too, as a path tracer's accumulation is: the denoiser never gets past its first frame and the glossy floor shows the tracer's raw noise while the camera moves. (Leaving out the reset at a cut barely shows with `denoiser/ffx`, which rejects history whose depth and normals disagree; it is still what a cut needs.)

**Try this.**

- Watch the floor through the camera moves and the cuts: steady, no shimmer
- Tick Reset history on camera moves: the floor sparkles until the shot ends
- Pause: the image holds still (the rest-flicker check holds it to the quick start's bound)

### Upscaling

TAA / upscaling after the composite with @pmndrs/upscaler, jitter wired through.

**What it shows.** Rendering the scene and its reflections below the display size and upscaling after the composite: fewer pixels for every reflection pass, and anti-aliasing with it.

**Compare.** Upscaler off: every pass at the display resolution, no anti-aliasing

**Try this.**

- Tick Upscaler off: display resolution, no anti-aliasing (edges and the last shimmer return)
- Set Upscale to Performance (2x): a quarter of the pixels; orbit and let go
- Set Upscale to Native AA: the upscaler as temporal anti-aliasing only

### Device limits

A plain new WebGPURenderer(): the second lit pass, traceError when ray tracing does not fit.

**What it shows.** What changes on a renderer with WebGPU's default limits: a second scene draw for the lit image, estimated specular weights, and ray tracing turning itself off (with `traceError`) when it cannot fit. Nothing fails.
