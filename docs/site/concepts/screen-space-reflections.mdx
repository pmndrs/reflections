Screen-space reflections (SSR) find reflections in the image you already rendered. For each glossy pixel, the
tracer shoots a ray along the mirrored view direction and marches it through the depth buffer until it hits
something. The colour at the hit is the reflection.

This library's tracer is a port of a hierarchical stochastic screen-space tracer from AMD's open-source samples
(attribution in [NOTICE](https://github.com/pmndrs/reflections/blob/main/NOTICE)). In plain words:

- **Hierarchical.** It builds a pyramid of the depth buffer and takes big steps over empty space, small steps near
  surfaces. That is what makes a long ray cheap.
- **Stochastic.** Rough surfaces send a random ray inside the specular lobe. One ray per pixel is noisy, and a
  denoiser cleans it up over a few frames. That is why SSR and the [denoiser](/concepts/denoising-and-upscaling) go together.
- **Classified first.** Pixels rougher than `roughnessThreshold` (0.447 perceptual by default) skip the trace and
  take the probe or the environment. A rough surface has a soft reflection that a probe gives well and for free.

## What you get

For every pixel the pass gives you three textures: the reflected radiance, the world distance to the hit, and a
confidence in the range 0 to 1. Confidence is 0 for a miss and fades toward the screen edge, so the
[hybrid resolve](/concepts/hybrid-resolve) knows how much to trust the hit.

## What it cannot see

SSR only knows what is on screen. It misses:

- anything off screen, which is why the [probes](/concepts/reflection-probes) are the fallback;
- anything hidden behind a nearer object, such as the back of a thing or the floor under a table;
- very thin objects, which the march can step over. `settings.maxCandidates` (1 to 8, default 1) makes the march look
  past a rejected hit and keep going, which helps for a thin post in front of a wall. It costs about 0.25 ms at 8.

For the misses that matter, add the [ray-traced fallback](/concepts/ray-traced-fallback).

## Using it

{/* snippet: settings */}
```ts
ssr.settings.roughnessThreshold = 0.5; // trace rougher surfaces (more rays, more noise)
ssr.settings.samplesPerQuad = 2; // two rays per 2x2 quad on glossy pixels
ssr.settings.maxCandidates = 4; // look past thin objects in front of a wall
```

The settings are read every frame, so you can change them live. The main ones:

| Setting | What it trades |
| --- | --- |
| `roughnessThreshold` | Higher traces rougher surfaces: more rays and more noise for the denoiser. |
| `samplesPerQuad` (1, 2, 4) | More rays per 2x2 quad, less noise, more cost. |
| `maxCandidates` (1 to 8) | Looks past thin occluders. 1 is the original behaviour. |
| `reflectionResolution` (`'full'`, `'half'`) | Half resolution traces and denoises a quarter of the pixels and upsamples. |

See [Performance and quality](/guides/performance-and-quality) and the [settings reference](/reference/settings).
